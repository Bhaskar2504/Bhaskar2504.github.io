Flagship Technical Project

Industrial GenAI Reliability Copilot

GenAI · Reliability Engineering · v0.2

Turning reliability expertise into a safe, testable GenAI workflow.

A public engineering project showing how Industrial AI, Asset Performance Management and reliability reasoning can be converted into structured diagnostic outputs with versioned prompts, schemas, synthetic evidence, automated evaluation, safety checks and human escalation.

Current stage: v0.2 — Evaluation Baseline. The reproducible synthetic baseline contains 8 cases: 7 pass and 1 deliberately retained failure that exposes a contradiction-handling weakness. Structured-output validity and evidence citation-ID accuracy are 100%; abstention and human-escalation accuracy are 87.5%. RAG with citations remains planned for v0.3.

Independent Cases

Five different engineering problems.

01Rotor Dynamics

The turbine vibration that followed load

Absolute vibration remains inside its alarm limit, but the rotor becomes more sensitive to load and steam-condition changes. The story explores why the changing relationship matters and which rotor-dynamic mechanisms should be investigated.

Read case → 02Thermodynamics

The air preheater that quietly changed the boiler

Air outlet temperature, flue-gas oxygen and draft behaviour begin telling a common thermal story. The case separates heat-recovery deterioration and air-ingress reasoning from a simple isolated temperature alarm.

Read case → 03Cooling & Flow

When pressure and temperature fall together

A cooling-water loop shows a coordinated change in pressure and temperature. The case uses the relationship to investigate a possible change in flow path rather than treating either measurement independently.

Read case → 04Pump Hydraulics

The feedwater pump and a changing suction path

Increasing suction-side restriction and deteriorating hydraulic performance shift attention toward the suction system. Motor current is treated as a supporting signal whose behaviour must be interpreted at the actual pump operating point.

Read case → 05Instrumentation

The temperature that looked dangerous—but was alone

One winding-temperature indication moves sharply while peer measurements, cooling conditions and electrical loading remain stable. The disagreement becomes evidence for an instrumentation hypothesis that still requires engineering verification.

How the Cases Are Built

Evidence before conclusions.

1. Engineering context

What the asset does, what operating condition matters, and which system boundary should be considered.

2. Signal pattern

Which variables changed, which stayed stable, and whether the important information lies in the relationship between them.

3. Physical interpretation

Which mechanical, thermal, hydraulic, electrical or instrumentation mechanisms could plausibly produce the observed behaviour.

4. Engineering response

What should be validated, inspected, trended or planned before the hypothesis is treated as a confirmed diagnosis.
