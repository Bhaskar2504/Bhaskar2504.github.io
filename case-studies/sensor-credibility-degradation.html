Case structure: measurement credibility → independent evidence → chronology → intervention → recurrence → verification.

01 · Evidence chronology

The sensor problem was only one part of the story.

Once the asset history is reconstructed, the questionable measurement sits inside a wider mechanical pattern.

Early evidence
Vibration becomes erratic or intermittently elevated.

This is independent of the later questionable temperature channel and establishes a separate mechanical signal family.

Instrumentation
A bearing-temperature measurement is flagged as unreliable more than once.

Confidence in that channel drops. The correct response is to investigate the measurement path while retaining other asset evidence.

Continued condition
Vibration hunting/spiking appears again at related measurement points.

The recurring mechanical pattern weakens the argument that the entire condition story is only an instrumentation artifact.

Adverse event
A later event is associated with the same bearing system.

This does not prove that the earlier bad-quality sensor caused or predicted the event, but it makes dismissal of the mechanical evidence unsafe.

After intervention
A related vibration symptom returns.

Recurrence means the repaired state should be verified rather than assumed healthy.

Credibility logic

Bad data can coexist with real degradation.

Sensor credibility and equipment condition are separate questions. A weak sensor can make diagnosis harder without making the asset healthy.

Engineering interpretation

Measurement issue + possible real mechanical degradation

The correct diagnosis is not “bad sensor” or “bad bearing” from one record. It is that the sensor path needs verification while the independent mechanical evidence continues to justify asset inspection.

1Check transmitter/RTD wiring, channel quality and calibration history.
2Compare orthogonal or peer vibration channels.
3Review lubrication, alignment, bearing condition and recent intervention details.
4Confirm whether the post-intervention vibration signature is the same mechanism.
APM implicationSensor-health logic should sit beside failure-mode logic.

A model should be able to say “measurement credibility is degraded” without suppressing independent evidence that still points to equipment risk.

Scope: This case does not claim that the questionable temperature measurement predicted the later event, nor that recurring vibration proves a specific bearing failure mode. It shows why instrumentation credibility must be evaluated alongside independent asset evidence.
