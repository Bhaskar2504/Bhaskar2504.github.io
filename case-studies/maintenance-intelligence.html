01 · Method

From fragmented records to a trustworthy physical history.

The difficult part is not finding a keyword. It is reconstructing what happened to the physical asset when records, hierarchy and maintenance wording disagree.

01Normalize recordsStandardize descriptions, event types, asset references and obvious duplicates.
02Rebuild asset historyLink warnings, adverse events, repairs and follow-up work into one chronology.
03Group failure modesSeparate repeated mechanisms from unrelated maintenance volume.
04Compare peersUse sister assets, parallel systems and non-events as evidence.
05Test hypothesesCheck physical plausibility, sensor credibility and repair effectiveness.
06Translate to APMImprove hierarchy, monitoring logic, validation and workflow.
07Define actionInspect, test, monitor, correct data or escalate.
08Preserve uncertaintyKeep fact, hypothesis and unresolved evidence visibly separate.
02 · Patterns

What becomes visible only after the records are connected.

These are transferable reliability patterns, not source-specific disclosures.

Precursor chainsWarnings before later adverse events

Low-severity records can become meaningful when they form a coherent asset sequence.

Repair effectivenessDid the intervention actually change the condition?

Recurrence after repair is evidence to revisit the intervention or diagnosis.

Fleet behaviourSame signature across parallel machines

Repeated modes across peers shift attention toward shared design, operation or maintenance influences.

Data qualityHierarchy, duplicates and free text matter

Structured fields can fragment history while duplicate work can inflate failure counts.

Hidden burdenReliability problems that never trip the plant

Recurring corrective work can consume maintenance capacity without a forced outage.

ReadinessStandby equipment must prove it can perform

Installed and available are not the same as demonstrated readiness.

05 · Engineering discipline

What the framework does not allow.

Do notTreat every ticket as a unique failureDuplicates, follow-up jobs and cross-tagged work distort counts.
Do notTurn recurrence directly into root causeRepeated behaviour strengthens a hypothesis; verification is still required.
DoUse contradicting evidenceA stable peer, absent symptom or failed hypothesis can narrow the search space.
DoKeep data quality inside diagnosisSensor credibility, hierarchy quality and event duplication affect the conclusion itself.
