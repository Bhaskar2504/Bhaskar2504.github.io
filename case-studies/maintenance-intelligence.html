Maintenance records are engineering evidence—not just work history.

01 · Method

From maintenance history to reliability intelligence.

The difficult part is not finding a keyword. It is reconstructing a trustworthy sequence around the physical asset.

01Normalize recordsStandardize descriptions, event types, asset references and obvious duplicates.
02Rebuild asset historyLink warnings, events, repairs and follow-up work into one chronology.
03Group failure modesSeparate recurring mechanisms from unrelated maintenance volume.
04Compare peersUse sister assets, parallel systems and non-events as supporting or contradicting evidence.
05Test hypothesesCheck physical plausibility, instrumentation credibility and repair effectiveness.
06Translate to APMImprove hierarchy, monitoring logic, failure-mode coverage and validation workflow.
07Define actionInspection, testing, targeted monitoring, data correction or escalation.
08Preserve uncertaintyKeep confirmed fact, diagnostic hypothesis and unresolved evidence visibly separate.
02 · Core patterns

What becomes visible when records are connected.

These are transferable reliability patterns—not source-specific disclosures.

Precursor chainsWarnings before later adverse events

Repeated low-severity observations can become meaningful when they form a coherent sequence around the same asset or subsystem.

Repair effectivenessDid the intervention actually change the condition?

Recurrence after repair is evidence to review the intervention, the diagnosis or the underlying mechanism.

Fleet behaviourSame signature across parallel machines

Repeated failure modes across comparable assets shift the question toward shared design, operating or maintenance influences.

Data qualityHierarchy, duplicates and free text matter

Structured fields can fragment the true asset history; duplicate work can inflate failure counts; free text may contain the missing linkage.

Hidden burdenReliability problems that never trip the plant

Chronic corrective work can consume maintenance capacity even when no single event causes an outage.

ReadinessStandby equipment must prove it can perform

Installed and available are not the same as ready. Proof-test and load-test behaviour matter.

05 · Engineering discipline

What this framework does not allow.

The value is in disciplined interpretation, not aggressive claims.

Do notTreat every maintenance ticket as a unique failureDuplicate records, follow-up jobs and cross-tagged work can distort failure counts.
Do notTurn recurrence directly into root causeRepeated behaviour creates a stronger hypothesis; engineering verification is still required.
DoUse contradicting evidenceA sister asset without the same symptom, a stable peer signal or a failed hypothesis can narrow the search space.
DoKeep data quality inside the diagnosisSensor credibility, hierarchy quality and event duplication affect the reliability conclusion itself.
