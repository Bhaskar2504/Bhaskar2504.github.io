Engineering-Driven AI Diagnostics

Combining equipment behaviour, failure mechanisms, process relationships, and analytics to improve diagnosis beyond black-box anomaly detection.

Detecting that something is unusual is only the first step. The harder question is why it is unusual. Engineering-driven diagnostics uses the physical relationships around an asset to turn a statistical deviation into a focused engineering investigation.

An anomaly is evidence, not a root cause

A model may show that vibration, temperature, pressure or current has departed from expected behaviour. That does not prove a specific failure mode. The diagnostic process must compare the pattern with equipment physics, process conditions, known failure mechanisms and other available measurements.

How the reasoning is built

Signal relationship

Which measurements changed together, and which expected relationships weakened or reversed?

Operating context

Was the asset starting, stopping, changing load, switching duty or operating under a different process condition?

Failure mechanism

Which mechanical, thermal, hydraulic, electrical or control mechanism could plausibly create the observed pattern?

Contradicting evidence

Which measurements do not support the hypothesis, and what alternative explanations should remain open?

Why multivariable context matters

Industrial assets rarely fail through one isolated variable. A hydraulic restriction may affect pressure, flow and pump behaviour. A heat-transfer problem may change temperature relationships and downstream loading. A sensor fault may appear as one measurement disagreeing with otherwise consistent peer signals. The relationships often carry more diagnostic information than any single value.

The role of AI

AI can help learn expected behaviour, rank deviations and surface patterns across large amounts of time-series data. Engineering knowledge remains important for deciding whether the pattern represents a genuine equipment issue, process behaviour, instrumentation problem or another operating condition.
