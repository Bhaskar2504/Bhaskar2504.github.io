Power & Process Applications

Applying reliability, performance, and analytics methods across thermal power, CCGT, plant auxiliaries, electrical systems, and process assets.

Industrial analytics becomes useful when it respects the physics and operating purpose of the equipment. Pumps, fans, turbines, boilers, electrical systems and process loops all produce data, but the relationships that matter are different for each system.

Start with the plant system

An asset should not be interpreted in isolation from the process it serves. A pump is connected to suction and discharge systems. A fan responds to draft and flow resistance. A turbine responds to steam conditions and load. Electrical equipment responds to loading, voltage, current, power factor and cooling. The system boundary determines which data is meaningful.

Application areas

Rotating equipment

Pumps, fans, compressors and turbines: vibration, hydraulic or aerodynamic behaviour, temperatures, load and process response.

Thermal systems

Boilers, HRSGs, heat exchangers and steam cycles: heat transfer, efficiency, temperature approach and operating losses.

Electrical systems

Motors, generators and plant electrical distribution: current, voltage, power, loading and equipment-specific electrical behaviour.

Process assets

Valves, cooling loops, water systems and other process equipment where flow, pressure, temperature and control response interact.

Why domain knowledge changes analytics

The same analytical method can give very different value depending on how signals are selected and interpreted. Engineering equations, operating limits, equipment curves, control logic and known failure mechanisms help determine what should be calculated, normalized or compared.

The common principle

Across power and process applications, the objective remains consistent: understand expected physical behaviour, observe how the actual system differs, and translate that difference into a useful engineering decision.
