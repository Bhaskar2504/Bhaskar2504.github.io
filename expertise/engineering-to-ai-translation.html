Engineering-to-AI Translation

Converting plant-domain problems into clear requirements that AI, ML, product, and software teams can implement.

Industrial AI projects often fail at the boundary between domain knowledge and implementation. Engineers describe the plant problem in operational language, while data science and software teams need measurable inputs, logic, constraints and expected outputs. Translation connects those two worlds.

Start with the engineering question

The first step is to define what decision needs to improve. Is the problem early failure detection, poor efficiency, unreliable instrumentation, operating-window control, process forecasting or diagnosis? The technical solution should follow from that question rather than from a preferred algorithm.

Translate the problem into implementable pieces

System boundary

Define the asset, upstream and downstream influences, operating states and relevant interfaces.

Signals & calculations

Specify raw measurements, calculated parameters, units, dependencies and data-quality expectations.

Expected behaviour

Describe what should happen physically when load, speed, temperature, pressure or another driver changes.

Decision output

Define what the user needs to see: anomaly, diagnosis, ranking, forecast, KPI, explanation or recommended investigation.

Why this matters

A technically correct algorithm can still solve the wrong problem if the engineering requirement is vague. Clear translation reduces ambiguity around tag mapping, calculations, asset hierarchy, failure logic, operating context and acceptance criteria.

The bridge between teams

Plant engineers understand the process and failure mechanisms. Data scientists understand models and statistical behaviour. Product and software teams understand workflows and implementation constraints. Strong industrial solutions emerge when those perspectives are converted into a common technical language.
