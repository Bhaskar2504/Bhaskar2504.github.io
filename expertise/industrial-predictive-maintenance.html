Industrial Predictive Maintenance

Using industrial data, engineering knowledge, and AI to identify developing equipment problems before they become failures.

Predictive maintenance is most useful when it does more than detect an unusual signal. The objective is to recognize a developing change early enough for engineers to understand it, validate it and decide what action is justified.

From alarms to developing behaviour

Traditional monitoring often asks whether a measurement has crossed a limit. Predictive monitoring adds a second question: has the relationship between operating condition and equipment response begun to change? A vibration value, bearing temperature, pressure, flow or current may still be individually acceptable while its behaviour relative to load, speed, ambient condition or another process variable is no longer normal.

The engineering workflow

1. Establish context

Understand the asset, operating modes, shutdowns, startups and process constraints before interpreting the data.

2. Identify useful signals

Select measurements that describe both the equipment response and the conditions driving that response.

3. Detect credible change

Look for persistent deviations, changing relationships and multivariable patterns rather than isolated noise.

4. Translate into action

Connect the pattern to plausible failure mechanisms, validate with engineering evidence and plan the next step.

What makes industrial predictive maintenance difficult

  • Plant data contains noise, missing values, bad sensors and changing operating regimes.
  • The same absolute value can mean different things at different loads or process conditions.
  • An anomaly is not automatically a failure diagnosis.
  • Useful alerts need enough persistence and context to avoid overwhelming plant teams.

Where engineering knowledge matters

Engineering knowledge provides the causal frame around the data. It helps determine which signals should move together, which calculated parameters are physically meaningful, which failure modes are plausible and what evidence is needed before recommending maintenance.
